# Tutoring Business Design Document

*Last updated: May 2026*

---

## 1\. Business Overview

**Business Name:** Dublin Tutoring Association 

**What We Are:** A small, personalized academic tutoring and college readiness practice founded by two recent UC graduates based in Dublin, CA. We serve high school students in the Tri-Valley area (Dublin, Pleasanton, San Ramon) through 1:1 tutoring, group sessions, and structured college readiness guidance.

**The Founding Story:** Ayush Bandopadhyay (B.S. CS/Game Design, Statistics Minor — UC Santa Cruz) and Ayush Bakhandi (B.S. Molecular Biology, Minor in Global Public Health — UC Berkeley) have tutored and mentored throughout high school and college. As family friends increasingly sought their guidance on academics, college applications, and class selection, they recognized two things: there is a lack of accessible, high-quality guidance from people who have recently lived the student experience, and professional alternatives charge rates that put that guidance out of reach for many families. DTA exists to close that gap.

**Core Philosophy:** Every student is different. Our job is to understand what a student actually needs — not sell them a package — and deliver focused, high-quality help that respects their time and schedule.

**Structure:** Currently a 2-person practice. Open to bringing on additional tutors as demand grows, provided quality standards are maintained.

## 2\. Target Market

**Geography:** Dublin, Pleasanton, and San Ramon (Tri-Valley, CA)

**Primary Students:** High school students, grades 9–12, who are motivated or want to be — self-driven, already enrolled in rigorous coursework (APs, honors), and looking for support that meets them where they are. Not students who need to be pushed; students who want to go further.

**Secondary Students:** Middle school students, grades 6–8. College readiness begins early — course selection, study habits, and academic identity are shaped in middle school. Marketed as 6–12, but operational focus is 9–12.

**The Parent Profile:**

- High-income, highly educated Tri-Valley families  
- Strong representation from South Asian and East Asian communities, where academic achievement is culturally prioritized  
- Already spending on enrichment — these parents know the market and are frustrated by rigid, expensive programs  
- Core fear: their child is falling behind peers, not reaching their potential, or will not get into a competitive university  
- Core frustration with existing options: too expensive, too structured, treats every student the same

**What They Are NOT:**

- Families looking for remedial help or basic grade recovery  
- Families who want a fixed curriculum or weekly class to outsource their child's development  
- Students who need to be sold on caring about school

**Why They'll Choose Us Over Competitors:**

- We are recent UC graduates — we went through this process recently and know what competitive admissions and rigorous coursework looks like right now  
- We support what students are already doing, not a new program layered on top  
- We are significantly more affordable than C2 ($70–100/hr) and Huntington ($67–99/hr) without sacrificing quality  
- True 1:1 sessions — not 3:1 "small groups" rebranded as personalized  
- Accessible and trusted — parents in our community already know people like us

**Community Channel:** The South Asian community in the Tri-Valley is a primary trust and referral network. This informs our early marketing channels (WhatsApp parent groups, community word-of-mouth) but is not made explicit in public-facing brand messaging, which remains inclusive and demographic-neutral.

**Competitive Landscape Summary:**

| Provider | Model | Price Range | Key Weakness |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| C2 Education | Package-based, small group | $2,000–$6,000+ | Expensive, rigid, impersonal |
| Huntington | Hourly \+ packages | $67–99/hr | High diagnostic fees, long commitments |
| Sylvan | Courses \+ hourly | $50+/session | Curriculum-based, not personalized |
| Kumon | Monthly subscription | $174/subject/month | Worksheet-only, no college prep |
| Steps Tutoring | Group classes | $55/hr (group) | Fixed schedule, not 1:1 |
| Private Tutors | Hourly | $60–150/hr | No college readiness, inconsistent quality |

*Research item: Conduct parent interviews during free consultations to validate price sensitivity and confirm which competitor pain points resonate most.*

## 3\. Services Offered

### Service Tiers

**1\. Free Initial Consultation (30 minutes, online)** Structured intake conversation — not a sales call. Goal is to understand the student's situation, match them to the right tutor, and determine next steps. Followed by an email summary within 48 hours. Full intake question framework in Section 7\.

**2\. 1:1 Private Tutoring Sessions (1 hour)** Core service. Fully personalized to the student's actual coursework, goals, and schedule. Sessions are booked in advance on a recurring or as-needed basis. No fixed curriculum — the student brings what they need help with.

*Can cover:*

- Subject-specific help (math, science, English, history, etc.)  
- AP/honors coursework support  
- College application essays and personal statement guidance  
- Course selection and academic planning  
- Study skills and habits

*Tutor specialization:*

- Ayush Bandopadhyay (UCSC — CS, Game Design, Statistics): STEM, CS, quantitative subjects, tech-track college prep  
- Ayush Bakhandi (UC Berkeley — Molecular Biology): Life sciences, pre-med track, biology, chemistry, research-oriented college prep  
- Both tutors handle general academics and college readiness; major-specific college counseling is matched to the relevant tutor

**3\. Office Hours (Free perk for active 1:1 clients)** Fixed weekly time slots — same days and times each week, similar to university office hours. Students can drop in with quick questions, get feedback on drafts, or clarify something from a session. Not a replacement for a full session.

- Capped at 30 minutes per student per week  
- Format: online (default) or in-person if feasible  
- Available to currently enrolled 1:1 clients only  
- Specific days/times TBD based on tutor availability

**4\. Group Tutoring Sessions (Interest-based, future offering)** Not available at launch. Will be introduced once sufficient client base exists and common needs are identified through consultations and 1:1 sessions. Groups are formed organically around shared subject needs or college prep timelines — not preset curricula.

- Size: 3–6 students  
- Discounted rate compared to 1:1 (exact pricing TBD — see Section 4\)  
- Subject and format determined by cohort interest

### Format & Location

- **Online:** Default for consultations and flexible for tutoring sessions (video call platform TBD — Google Meet, Zoom, etc.)  
- **In-person:** Our homes.

### Capacity (Current)

- 8 hours/week per tutor \= 16 hours/week total  
- Estimated allocation: \~2 hrs/week office hours, \~2 hrs/week consultations, \~12 billable 1:1 hours/week  
- Maximum active clients at launch: approximately 10–12 students  
- *This ceiling should inform if we need more tutors*

## 4\. Pricing Strategy

**Philosophy:** Meaningfully undercut the Tri-Valley market without devaluing the service. Competitors charge $70–100/hr for 1:1 tutoring. We charge $50/hr — roughly 30% below market — made possible by zero overhead (no physical center, no admin staff, Zoom-based by default). Affordability is a core part of our value proposition, not an afterthought.

### 1:1 Private Tutoring

| Option | Rate | Details |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Hourly (pay as you go) | $50/hr | Default entry option, low commitment |
| 10-Session Package | $450 | $45/hr — 10% discount, expires in 3 months |
| 20-Session Package | $800 | $40/hr — 20% discount, expires in 6 months |

### Group Tutoring (Future Offering)

- Per-student rate: $25–30/hr (TBD once cohort forms and format is defined)  
- Minimum 3 students, maximum 6  
- Exact pricing confirmed in Section 3 once group sessions launch

### Discounts

**Sibling Discount:** 10% off the lower-priced sibling's sessions. Applies to both hourly and package rates. Example: two siblings on hourly \= one at $50/hr, one at $45/hr.

**No other standing discounts** — referral incentives and promotional pricing may be introduced later but are not part of the base model at launch.

### Cancellation Policy

- Reschedule or cancel with **24+ hours notice** at no charge  
- Same-day cancellations or no-shows: charged **50% of session rate ($25)**  
- This policy is communicated clearly at consultation and included in the follow-up email

### Competitive Positioning

| Provider | 1:1 Rate | vs. DTA ($50/hr) |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| C2 Education | \~$70/hr (bundled) | 29% cheaper |
| Huntington | $67–99/hr | 25–50% cheaper |
| Sylvan | $50+/session | Comparable, far more personalized |
| Private Tutors | $60–150/hr | Cheaper and more structured |

### Revenue Snapshot (Current Capacity)

- 12 billable hours/week at $50/hr \= **$600/week**  
- Monthly combined revenue at full capacity: **\~$2,400/month**  
- Per tutor: **\~$1,200/month**  
- *Note: Package sales will smooth revenue week-to-week. Goal is to convert hourly clients to packages within the first month.*

## 5\. Brand & Positioning

**Business Name:** DTA

**One-Liner:** "We help students with their academics, planning, and college readiness."

**Elevator Pitch (parent at a school event):** "We're DTA — a small tutoring practice run by two recent UC grads. We work 1:1 with high school students on whatever they actually need — whether that's a specific class, college planning, or application essays. We're not a tutoring center and we don't add to a student's schedule. We support what they're already doing and help them do it better."

**Brand Adjectives:** Confident. Reassured. Satisfied. *These are how a student or parent should feel after every interaction with DTA — not anxious, not overwhelmed, not sold to.*

**Tone & Voice:**

- Warm but not casual  
- Knowledgeable but not condescending  
- Honest — we tell families what students actually need, not what generates the most sessions  
- Never fear-based — we do not market to parental anxiety. We market to potential.

**Primary Identity:** Mentor \+ Coach We are not peers pretending to be friends, and we are not distant professionals. We are recent graduates who just lived this experience — we know what competitive college admissions looks like *right now*, what APs actually demand, and what students need to succeed. That recency is our credibility.

**Key Differentiators:**

- Recent UC graduates (UCSC, UC Berkeley) — not career tutors disconnected from the current academic landscape  
- True 1:1 sessions — personalized to the student's actual coursework and goals  
- Support layer, not supplemental curriculum — we work with what students are already doing  
- Genuinely affordable — $50/hr vs. $70–100/hr market rate  
- Mentor relationship — families get two people who actually care about outcomes, not a franchise

**What DTA Is Not:**

- A test prep factory  
- Another class on a student's schedule

**Visual & Naming Direction (TBD):**

- Modern, minimal aesthetic — nothing childish or overly corporate  
- Color palette should feel elevated and calm — navy, slate, warm white, or similar  
- No mascots, no stars/checkmarks, no generic academic imagery  
- Logo should work as a wordmark — "DTA" alone is enough

## 6\. Marketing & Acquisition

**Launch Target:** June 2026 **Approach:** Warm community-first. Start with trusted networks before any cold outreach. Build word-of-mouth before spending anything on design or distribution.

---

### Channel Strategy

**1\. WhatsApp Parent Groups (Primary — Launch Channel)**

- Access through family connections and Agomoni (Indian cultural nonprofit) network  
- Messages should feel personal, not promotional — ideally introduced by a trusted parent who vouches for DTA, not a cold drop  
- Message format: brief, warm, honest. Who we are, what we offer, how to book a free consultation. No hard sell.  
- Follow up with a clean one-page PDF (not a flyer) that parents can forward  
- *This is the highest-trust channel and should be activated first*

**2\. Agomoni Network (Primary — Community Channel)**

- Existing relationship with an established Indian cultural nonprofit in the Tri-Valley  
- Leverage for word-of-mouth, community event presence, and direct parent outreach  
- Potential to offer a group info session or Q\&A at an Agomoni event as a soft launch  
- *Do not over-commercialize this channel — the relationship is an asset, treat it carefully*

**3\. Nextdoor (Secondary)**

- Posted through parents' verified neighborhood accounts — must read as a genuine personal recommendation, not an ad  
- Parents write the post in their own voice: "My son and his friend just started a tutoring practice, they're both recent UC grads and helped \[X\] — highly recommend reaching out"  
- Avoid promotional language, logos, or pricing in the post itself — drive to a consultation booking  
- *Risk: Nextdoor users flag posts that feel like ads. Keep it human.*

**4\. Physical Flyers (Contingency — If Online Channels Underperform)**

- Targeted distribution: libraries (Dublin, Pleasanton, San Ramon), community centers, and select local businesses frequented by the target demographic  
- Direct mail to specific neighborhoods as a last resort if digital outreach stalls  
- Flyer design handled in-house — must match DTA brand: clean, minimal, professional  
- *Do not lead with flyers. They are expensive in time and attention relative to warm digital outreach.*

**5\. Referral Program (Future — Post Launch)**

- Once first cohort of clients is established, introduce a referral incentive  
- Proposed structure: refer a new family who completes a paid session, receive one free session  
- Keep it simple — no tracking software needed at this scale, honor system with logged referrals  
- *Activate only after 5+ active clients. Too early and there's no one to refer.*

---

### Messaging Principles

- Lead with who you are, not what you charge  
- Emphasize recency: "We just went through this" is more compelling than credentials alone  
- Always drive to the free consultation — lowest barrier to entry, highest conversion opportunity

### Launch Success Metrics (June 2026\)

- Consultations booked: **8–10**  
- Paying clients by end of June: **5–6**  
- Paying clients by end of Summer: **10–12 (full capacity)**

## 7\. Operations

### Scheduling

- **Platform:** Notion  
- Clients see one booking link, select available slot, automatically assigned to correct tutor based on subject/fit determined at consultation  
- Synced to both tutors' Google Calendars  
- Session reminders sent automatically via Calendly (24hr and 1hr before)  
- *Decision needed before launch: set up Calendly and configure availability windows*

### Session Format

- **Platform:** Zoom (standardized — same link or personal room per tutor)  
- **Duration:** 1 hour (1:1 sessions), 30 minutes (consultations)  
- **In-person:** Available at Dublin, Pleasanton, or San Ramon public libraries — booked same way as online, location noted in confirmation email  
- Students should come prepared — bring the specific assignment, essay, problem set, or question they need help with

### Office Hours

- Fixed weekly time slots — same days and times every week  
- Communicated to active clients at onboarding  
- Drop-in via Zoom or in-person at a designated library  
- Free  
- Not bookable — first come, first served within the window  
- *Specific days/times to be set by both tutors before launch based on shared availability*

### Payment

- **Platform:** Business Venmo  
- **Policy:** All payments collected upfront before sessions begin  
- Hourly: payment before each session or weekly in advance  
- 10-session package ($450): paid in full at booking  
- 20-session package ($800): two installments — $400 at booking, $400 after session 10  
- Sibling discount applied at time of booking, reflected in payment request  
- No refunds on packages — unused sessions may be transferred or extended with 2 weeks notice  
- Cancellation fee ($25) charged via Zelle within 48 hours of no-show

### Student Tracking (Internal)

- **Platform:** Google Drive  
- One note per student, consistently structured:  
  - Student profile (grade, school, courses, goals, college targets)  
  - Session log (date, topics covered, progress notes)  
  - Action items (what student should work on before next session)  
  - Parent context (communication notes, concerns raised)  
- Notes are internal — not shared with students or parents  
- Parents receive a brief written progress summary via email every 3–4 sessions

### Consultation Process (Intake Interview)

Assign tutor based on subject fit. One tutor per consultation.

**Before the call:**

- Family books via Calendly  
- Automated confirmation email sent with Zoom link and a short pre-consultation form asking: student name, grade, school, subjects needing help, and primary goal (academic support / college readiness / both)

**During the call — structured questions:**

*Academic Context:*

- What courses are you currently taking, and which are you finding most challenging?  
- What does your GPA look like, and are you happy with it?  
- Are you in any AP or honors courses? Which ones?

*Goals:*

- What do you want to get out of tutoring — better grades, college prep, essay help, or something else?  
- Do you have specific colleges in mind? What's your timeline?  
- Is there a particular subject or area you want to focus on first?

*Scheduling & Fit:*

- How many hours per week can you realistically commit to tutoring?  
- Have you worked with a tutor before? What worked or didn't work?  
- What does your weekly schedule look like — sports, extracurriculars, other commitments?

*Parent questions (if parent is present):*

- What are your main concerns about your student's academic trajectory?  
- What would success look like for you after 3 months with DTA?

**After the call:**

- Send follow-up email within 48 hours including:  
  - Summary of what was discussed  
  - Recommended service (1:1 hourly, package, or not the right fit — be honest)  
  - Pricing and next steps  
  - Calendly link to book first session if moving forward  
- If strong fit is clear during the call, first session can be booked immediately

### Communication Standards

- Primary channel: Email (formal updates, invoices, progress summaries)  
- Response time commitment: within 24 hours on weekdays  
- Do not let parent communication become informal or boundary-less — set expectations at onboarding

## 8\. Goals & Milestones

### Purpose

DTA is a side business for both founders. Goals reflect sustainable, quality-first growth — not aggressive scaling. Success is defined by student outcomes and reputation, not revenue maximization.

### What Success Looks Like (Year 1\)

- 6+ active paying clients  
- At least one group session running  
- A reputation in the Tri-Valley community for improving grades and making students feel prepared and confident  
- A clean, repeatable operation that doesn't overwhelm either tutor's primary commitments

**Year 1 (June 2027\)**

- [ ] Established local reputation in Dublin, Pleasanton, San Ramon  
- [ ] Word-of-mouth is primary acquisition channel — marketing spend near zero  
- [ ] 6+ active clients maintained consistently  
- [ ] At least one recurring group session per subject area  
- [ ] Student outcomes documented and usable as social proof for future marketing  
- [ ] Decision point: continue as-is, expand tutor roster, or wind down based on founders' other commitments

---

### Growth Guardrails

- **Do not exceed capacity before quality systems are in place** — a bad client experience in a tight-knit community spreads faster than a good one  
- **Hire only when at sustained capacity** — and only tutors whose expertise fills a gap (e.g. humanities specialist, test prep specialist)  
- **Stay local** — no plans to expand beyond Tri-Valley; depth of reputation in one community is more valuable than thin reach across many  
- **Let outcomes drive marketing** — the goal is to be known for results, not visibility

### Reputation Goal

*"DTA students feel more confident, more prepared, and more in control of their academic path."* This is the reputation to build toward — not a ranking, not a guarantee, not a statistic. A feeling that spreads through word of mouth.
